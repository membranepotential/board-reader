/camera — the phone at the board

Landscape frames are the phone as held. The layout mirrors the real page: the picture and the pill rail are two flex tracks that never overlap, and everything the page says — chips, banner, hint — lives in one stack along the bottom of the picture, because the top of the frame is the part being aimed. Three already-decided changes are drawn here as the design, not as options: the portrait hard block, the named calibrate refusal, and automatic label rotation.

Minimize button presses. Where the system can resolve something itself it resolves it, states what it decided in one quiet line, and leaves a one-tap escape hatch. The old press-rotate-until-right loop is the thing this replaces.

notes against the real UI

Where these mockups depart from today's build. The rail is more · position · detect board · calibrate · start, with rotate labels appearing as a sixth, warn-ringed pill only when a re-detection may have moved the labels. Automatic label rotation removes that pill's main reason to exist: the node returns label_rotation after every calibration, and today the page turns that into a sticky warning telling the operator to press rotate labels until a8 matches. Here the page just applies it and says so. The warn-ringed pill is still worth keeping for the one case the node cannot speak to — a re-detection after a sustained loss, with no calibration in between.
